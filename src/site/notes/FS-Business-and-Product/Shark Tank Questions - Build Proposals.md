# Shark Tank Questions - Build Proposals

**Official framing:** MTE SOC Engineering Leadership asks every team to address these seven
items in each proposal to take a work plan to **Build**. It is a leadership ask, not one
person's checklist — present it that way.

Originated with Todd Powell, who walks the list live during proposals and names what was
missed. Attribution below is provenance for my own use; it is deliberately absent from the
shared Confluence page.

## The Seven

| # | Action | What to cover |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ask clearly what is wanted** | "We are asking for approval to build" |
| 2 | **Explain the problem** | Short problem definition |
| 3 | **Demonstrate the solution** | Summary of the solution, with a visual |
| 4 | **Justify the opportunity** | Value proposition / hypotheses: will increase X by ?% or save costs |
| 5 | **Prove demand** | Who needs this, and how much |
| 6 | **Explain the numbers** | What data validates all of this |
| 7 | **Show why you can execute** | What is the cost and estimated time needed |

## How It Gets Applied

From the FamilySearch Assistant build proposal (Rhett Jorgensen and Scot Brooksby),
2026-09-14 — the first time the list was used in the open:

- **Give each item its own slide.** Rhett covered #4 but folded it into other slides. Todd:
  *"You didn't have a specific slide for it, which is what we'd like, but you kind of combined
  it with the other slides."*
- **#6 means an actual report, not a statistic.** Todd wants the **baseline report** on screen —
  current data next to expected future data — so changes are visible later. *"You had part of
  the data there, which is 1 out of 4, right? But you didn't have it in a report form."* If the
  system isn't built yet, a mockup is fine: *"It still is nice to have a visual of what you think
  the report's gonna be, even if it were in Excel or something like that."* The goal is habit:
  *"We want to get the teams in a habit of having those reports available in some way."*
- **#7 is the one teams skip.** Todd's framing: *"Think of this as you're going into Shark Tank
  and you're gonna get money… This one's gonna cost this much — how much man-days and/or how
  much time it's gonna cost… We believe this is the value we're gonna get, and here's how much
  it's gonna cost us."*
- **Phases are acceptable**, but each phase needs its own estimate and work plan.
- **The estimate belongs in Pilot.** *"That should be in Pilot, right in your work plan, in
  Pilot, in your build request. That's expected. So if you don't show it there, you need to show
  it in a slide."*
- **State your assumptions.** When Scot noted his estimate assumed MVP scope only, Todd:
  *"If that's the assumption you're making, then that's what you give."*

On 2026-09-14 the Assistant team covered items 1-5 and missed 6 and 7. Todd: *"Great job,
except the last two."*

## Where This Fits

Approval in the Weekly Engineering Leadership Meeting is the **first** gate. Per Lance's
invite: *"Once approved in this setting, you will be asked to present the proposal to the
Expanded SOC Leadership's 4pm Business Review."*

Todd has also reliably asked, outside the seven:

- Do you have the resources to do it?
- When will you start?
- Is Pilot updated with your start date and estimated end date based on these dev estimates?
- Do you already have the designs? (i.e. justify the UX days in the estimate)
